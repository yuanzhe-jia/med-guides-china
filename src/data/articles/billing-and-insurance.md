For international patients considering treatment in China, navigating the insurance landscape is one of the most important steps. The good news is that many global insurance providers have established strong networks with Chinese hospitals, making it easier than ever to access care without worrying about large upfront payments.

In China, medical payments for international patients generally fall into three categories: direct billing (cashless), where the hospital bills your insurer directly and you only pay deductibles or co-pays; pay-and-claim, where you pay upfront and seek reimbursement later; and hybrid models that combine both approaches. While direct billing is growing rapidly, roughly 80% of treatment scenarios in China—particularly in general public hospital departments—still require upfront payment followed by reimbursement. This reflects how China's public hospitals are financed rather than a system failure.

So which insurers actually offer direct billing in China? MSH China remains the most deeply entrenched player in the high-end medical market, with a network of over 1,318 directly contracted providers across the country. Their direct billing footprint covers major private international hospitals like United Family, Jiahui, and Parkway, as well as leading public hospital international departments including Peking Union Medical College Hospital, China-Japan Friendship Hospital, and Huashan Hospital. MSH offers a 24/7 bilingual hotline and real-time hospital lookup through their app.

Aetna International and Cigna Global are also widely accepted at international hospitals in major cities. Cigna, for instance, has direct billing with most international hospitals in Shenzhen, including United Family and Parkway Health. Bupa, while having exited direct insurance operations in mainland China as of 2026, continues to maintain services through Bupa Hong Kong and licensed partners, supporting premium private hospitals and select public hospital international departments. Ping An Health also offers private international plans with growing direct billing networks.

The direct billing process is straightforward: present your insurance card at registration, the hospital verifies coverage with your insurer, treatment is provided, and the hospital bills your insurer directly. You are only responsible for non-covered items. Some hospitals may request a deposit for major procedures, but this is typically refundable.

Before your trip, always confirm that your plan covers mainland China and check which hospitals are in your insurer's direct-billing network. Most international departments list their partner insurers on their websites. With the right insurance, your medical journey in China can be remarkably smooth—both clinically and financially.
