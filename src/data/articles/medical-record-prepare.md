Preparing your medical records properly is one of the most critical—and most overlooked—steps before traveling to China for treatment. In fact, incomplete or incorrectly formatted documentation is the single most common cause of delays for international patients. Chinese top-tier hospitals, such as Peking Union Medical College Hospital, operate at a scale where specialists may see hundreds of outpatients per day, and without complete records, your case simply cannot move forward.

So what do you need to bring? Your core documentation package should include pathology and biopsy reports, surgical summaries, a current medication list with dosages, and a treating physician's summary letter. But there is one requirement that catches most patients off guard: Chinese hospitals require raw DICOM imaging files from your CT, MRI, or PET-CT scans—not printed films, not JPEGs, not just the radiology report. You can obtain these from the radiology department where your scan was performed, usually on a CD or USB drive.

Translation is another essential piece. While some documents can be translated using high-quality machine translation with human review, critical documents like pathology reports and surgical summaries require professional medical translation. Expect to pay around $30–$80 per document, with a turnaround time of 24 to 48 hours. For surgical consent discussions, never rely on translation apps—their accuracy for medical terminology is only about 70–80%, and the risks are simply too high.

A final practical tip: ensure your name is spelled consistently with your passport across all translated documents to avoid registration delays. With proper preparation, your medical journey in China can begin smoothly and without unnecessary stress.
