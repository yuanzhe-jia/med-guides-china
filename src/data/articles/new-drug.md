For international patients seeking access to groundbreaking new drugs, China has created a unique and rapidly evolving pathway. The most direct route is through the Boao Lecheng International Medical Tourism Pilot Zone in Hainan Province, the country’s only “special medical zone”. Here, patients can access innovative pharmaceuticals and medical devices that have been approved abroad but are not yet available elsewhere in China. The zone operates under a “licensed medical” policy that allows the use of these imported drugs within its designated hospitals. The results have been remarkable: as of late 2025, the zone had introduced over 530 types of licensed drugs and devices, benefiting more than 200,000 patients. The average approval time for bringing in these international products has been compressed to just 1.6 days. This efficiency is transforming patient care. For instance, a Greek engineer diagnosed with a brain tumor was able to receive treatment with Vorasidenib, a new drug not yet available in his home country, at a hospital within the zone. In 2025, over 9,300 overseas patients from 14 countries and regions came to Lecheng for medical treatment. Beyond this special zone, China’s broader pharmaceutical landscape is also shifting. In 2025 alone, the country approved 76 Class 1 innovative new drugs, a record high. The nation has also become a major player in global drug development, with the total value of out-licensing deals for Chinese innovative drugs surpassing $135 billion in 2025. For patients in need of the latest therapies, China is increasingly becoming a viable and accessible destination.
