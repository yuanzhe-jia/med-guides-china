# Visa Guide for Medical Travelers to China

Planning to come to China for medical treatment? Understanding your visa options is the first step. This guide breaks down everything you need to know.

## Visa Options for Medical Travel

### Option 1: Visa-Free Transit (Short Stays)

If you're just coming for an initial consultation or evaluation, you might qualify for visa-free transit.

**Who qualifies**:
- Citizens of 53 countries (including US, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, EU countries, and many more)
- Staying in Beijing for **up to 144 hours (6 days)**
- Traveling to a third country after China (not returning directly to your departure country)

**How it works**:
1. Book a flight to Beijing with a connecting flight to a third country within 144 hours
2. Fill out the arrival/disembarkation card on the plane
3. At immigration, tell the officer "144-hour visa-free transit"
4. Show your onward ticket
5. Get your stamp and you're in!

**Important**: This is great for initial consultations and evaluations. If you think you might need longer, get a proper visa — extensions are possible but can be complicated.

### Option 2: S2 Visa (Medical Treatment)

For planned medical treatment that requires more than 6 days, you'll need an **S2 (Private Affairs) visa** with the purpose of medical treatment.

**How to apply**:

1. **First, get a hospital invitation letter**:
   - Contact the hospital's international department
   - Confirm your appointment and treatment plan
   - Request an official invitation letter for visa purposes
   - Hospitals usually provide this within 3-5 business days via email

2. **Prepare your documents**:
   - Valid passport (must be valid for at least 6 months with blank pages)
   - Visa application form (available on Chinese embassy/consulate websites)
   - Recent passport photo
   - Hospital invitation letter
   - Proof of travel arrangements (flight bookings)
   - Proof of accommodation (hotel reservations or invitation from a friend)
   - Proof of financial means (bank statements, etc.)
   - Medical records briefly describing your condition

3. **Submit your application**:
   - Apply at your nearest Chinese embassy, consulate, or visa application center
   - Processing time: Usually **4-7 business days** for standard service
   - Express service available (2-3 business days) for an extra fee
   - You can apply by mail or in person

**S2 Visa details**:
- Typically issued for **30, 60, or 90 days** per entry
- Can be **single-entry or multiple-entry**
- Can usually be extended in China if your treatment requires more time
- The hospital's international department can help with extension letters

### Option 3: Tourist Visa (L Visa)

For very short, simple consultations (under 30 days), some travelers use a standard tourist L visa. This is acceptable for initial evaluations, but if you're coming specifically for treatment, the S2 visa is more appropriate and reduces risk of any issues at immigration.

## Tips for Smooth Entry

1. **Carry your hospital invitation letter** with you — immigration may ask to see it
2. **Have the hospital's address and phone number** saved in your phone
3. **Be honest about your purpose of visit**: Say "medical consultation" or "medical treatment" — don't just say "tourism" if you're here for health reasons
4. **Keep your return flight details** easily accessible
5. **Travel insurance** with medical coverage is always a good idea

## Extending Your Stay in China

If your treatment takes longer than expected, you can usually extend your visa:

1. The hospital's international department will provide a **medical certificate** confirming you need more time
2. Apply at the local Exit-Entry Administration Bureau (usually 7-10 days before your visa expires)
3. Bring your passport, hospital letter, accommodation proof, and passport photos
4. Processing takes about 7 business days
5. Extensions of 30-90 days are common for medical reasons
