# Emergency Care in Beijing: What to Do When Minutes Matter

We hope you never need this guide — but if you do, knowing what to do can save precious time and reduce stress. Beijing has excellent emergency medical services, and as a foreign patient, you have several options.

## For Life-Threatening Emergencies: Call 120

**120** is China's national emergency ambulance number. Here's how it works:

### What to Say
Speak clearly and calmly:
1. *"English service requested, please."* (Many dispatchers in Beijing speak basic English)
2. Describe the emergency in simple terms: *"Heart attack," "Car accident," "Stroke symptoms"*
3. Give your exact location — street address, building, and room number if indoors
4. Mention that you're a foreigner

### What to Expect
- Ambulances typically arrive within **10-20 minutes** in urban areas
- Paramedics can provide basic life support on scene
- They'll transport you to the nearest appropriate hospital
- Cost: Approximately $15-$50 USD for the ambulance ride (pay on arrival or by card)

## Common Emergency Situations & What to Do

### Chest Pain / Possible Heart Attack
- **Call 120 immediately** — don't wait
- Sit down and rest
- If you have aspirin and are not allergic, chew one tablet (300mg)
- Ask to be taken to Anzhen Hospital or Fuwai Hospital (cardiac specialists)

### Stroke Symptoms (FAST)
- **Face**: Is one side drooping?
- **Arms**: Can both arms be raised?
- **Speech**: Is speech slurred or strange?
- **Time**: Call 120 right away — every minute matters
- Request Tiantan Hospital (neurosurgery specialist)

### Broken Bones / Orthopedic Injuries
- If severe (compound fracture, unable to move): call 120
- If you can move: go directly to Jishuitan Hospital ER (China's top orthopedic hospital)
- Keep the injured area immobilized

### Fever with Severe Symptoms
- If mild fever: rest and monitor
- If high fever (over 39°C / 102.2°F) with difficulty breathing, confusion, or severe pain: go to ER immediately
- Mention any recent travel history

## What to Bring to the ER

- **Passport** (for registration)
- **Insurance card** (if applicable)
- **List of current medications**
- **Any known allergies**
- **Emergency contact information**

## Medical Evacuation Insurance

If you're in China for an extended period or have a serious pre-existing condition, we strongly recommend travel insurance that includes **medical evacuation coverage**. This can cover the cost of flying you to your home country or a nearby international medical hub if needed.
