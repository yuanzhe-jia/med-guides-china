# How to Book a Doctor's Appointment in Beijing

Booking a doctor's appointment in a foreign country can feel overwhelming — but it doesn't have to be. This guide walks you through everything you need to know, step by step.

## Before You Start: Choose the Right Hospital

First, decide what kind of hospital best fits your needs:

- **Public hospital international departments** (推荐): Top-tier medical quality at reasonable prices, with English services. Best for most conditions.
- **Premium private hospitals**: Western-style experience, shorter waits, more languages, but higher prices. Best for routine care, obstetrics, and convenience.
- **Specialty hospitals**: The best choice for specific conditions (e.g., orthopedics at Jishuitan, neurosurgery at Tiantan).

## Booking Methods

### 1. Hospital International Department Hotline (Recommended)

The easiest and most reliable way for international patients is to call the hospital's international department directly.

- **What to expect**: English-speaking staff who specialize in helping foreign patients
- **What you'll need**:
  - Your passport number
  - A brief description of your symptoms or condition
  - Your preferred date and time
  - Any relevant medical records you can email in advance

**Pro tip**: Call 2-3 days in advance for routine visits. For popular specialists, book 1-2 weeks ahead.

### 2. Hospital Official WeChat Account

Most major hospitals have WeChat mini-programs for booking. This is convenient if you have a Chinese phone number and WeChat Pay.

However, many international departments also accept WeChat appointment requests through their official accounts — you can message them directly in English.

### 3. 114 Beijing Appointment Platform

The official city-wide appointment service (114) has some English support, but it can be hit-or-miss. For better results, use the hospital's direct international line instead.

### 4. Walk-in Appointments

Some international departments accept walk-ins, especially for urgent or same-day needs. But we always recommend calling ahead to avoid long waits or disappointment.

## What to Bring on Appointment Day

- **Your passport** (required for registration at international departments)
- **Your insurance card** (if you have direct billing coverage)
- **Previous medical records** (test results, imaging reports, etc. — translated if possible)
- **List of current medications**
- **Cash or credit card** (for registration fees if not using direct billing)

## Tips for a Smooth Experience

1. **Arrive 15-20 minutes early** to complete registration paperwork
2. **Ask for an English-speaking nurse** if you need help navigating the hospital
3. **Save the international department phone number** in your phone for follow-up questions
4. **Request bilingual receipts** if you'll need to file an insurance claim back home
